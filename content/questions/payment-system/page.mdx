<TLDR>
Every checkout carries an **idempotency key** claimed under a unique constraint, and a PSP timeout means **unknown**, settled by a deduplicated **webhook** or a status query. Money moves as append-only **double-entry ledger** transactions written with an **outbox** event, and daily **reconciliation** catches what slips through.
</TLDR>

A **correctness-under-failure** question, not a scale one. Networks time out after the work is done, PSPs send webhooks twice and out of order, customers double-tap. State the invariant first: every cent moves exactly once and is recorded exactly once.

## Clarifying questions

- **Parties?** A marketplace: customers pay, the platform takes a commission, merchants are paid out later.
- **Methods?** Cards answer in about a second; bank debits and some wallets confirm hours later, by webhook. Stored cards keep the PSP's token, never the card.
- **Charge now, or authorize now and capture on shipment?**
- **Currencies and refunds?** Who carries FX risk? Partial, several per payment, time-limited?
- **Volume and peak?** Black Friday, flash sales.

## Capacity estimate

| Quantity | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Payments | 10M/day ÷ 86,400 s | ≈ **116/s** average |
| Peak | ~10× on Black Friday | ≈ **1,160/s** |
| PSP calls in flight | 1,160/s × ~1 s latency (Little's law) | ≈ **1,160 concurrent** |
| Ledger entries | 4 per payment (charge, fee, commission) × 10M | **40M rows/day** |
| Row writes at peak | ~12 per payment (key, payment, ledger, outbox, event) × 1,160 | ≈ **14K rows/s** |
| Storage | 10M × 0.5 KB payments + 40M × 150 B entries | ≈ 11 GB/day, **4 TB/year** |
| Money moved | 10M × $50 average | **$500M/day** |

One tuned Postgres primary handles this; shard by `merchant_id` when it can't. Two numbers shape the design:

- **1,160 calls** are always waiting on the PSP, so hold no transaction or lock across one.
- At $500M a day, a 0.01% error rate is **1,000 wrong payments daily**. Correctness needs constraints, a ledger and reconciliation, not luck.

## API

| Method | Path | Body | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| POST | `/payments` | `Idempotency-Key` header; `orderId`, `amount`, `currency`, `paymentMethod` (PSP token) | `201` final, `202 processing` while unknown, `402` declined |
| GET | `/payments/:id` | | `id`, `status`, `amount`, `refunded` |
| POST | `/refunds` | `Idempotency-Key`; `paymentId`, `amount` | `201`, or `409` above the refundable amount |
| POST | `/webhooks/psp` | The PSP's signed event | `200` once stored, duplicates included |

The client makes the key (a UUID) once per checkout attempt and reuses it on retries; the server keeps keys at least 24 hours. Same key and body: the **stored** response, replayed. First request still running: `409`. Different body: `422`.

Cards go into the PSP's form and come back as a token, so **PCI DSS scope** shrinks to the pages embedding that form.

## Data model

<CodeBlock src="snippets/schema.sql" title="schema.sql" />

The payment's **state machine** is the spine:

- `created → processing`, committed before the PSP call.
- `processing → succeeded | failed | requires_action`, or `unknown` on timeout.
- `unknown → succeeded | failed`, settled by a webhook or status query, never a guess.
- `succeeded → partially_refunded → refunded`. `failed`, `canceled` and `refunded` are final.

Money lives in the **ledger**, not a balance column. A balance is the sum of entries: cache it with a version if reads get hot, and rebuild it from the entries when in doubt.

## Step-by-step answer

<Steps>
  <Step title="Keep card data out">
    The PSP's SDK swaps the card for a token in the browser; a breach of our database leaks no cards.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Claim an idempotency key in the same transaction">

One transaction claims the key and creates the payment. The unique key makes concurrent claims impossible:

```sql
BEGIN;
INSERT INTO idempotency_keys (customer_id, key, request_hash)
VALUES ($1, $2, $3)
ON CONFLICT (customer_id, key) DO NOTHING;      -- 0 rows: someone owns the key
-- if 0 rows: SELECT request_hash, status, response_code, response_body ...
--   hash differs     → ROLLBACK, 422
--   status 'done'    → ROLLBACK, replay the stored response
--   status 'started' → ROLLBACK, 409
INSERT INTO payments (id, ..., status) VALUES ($4, ..., 'processing');
COMMIT;
```

A racing insert waits for the commit, then inserts nothing. Check-then-insert in code races, and a Redis key drifts from the payment row. A partial unique index on `order_id` adds a second net: one live payment per order.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Never hold a transaction across the PSP call">
    Commit the claim, call the PSP with no transaction open, then record outcome, ledger and stored response in a second transaction. A crash in between leaves a `processing` row and `started` key; the recovery job asks the PSP. The intent was written first, so nothing is lost.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A timeout means unknown">
    Mark it `unknown` (never failed) and return `202`. Send the PSP your own key, never a **new** one, so a retry returns the original charge. Retry transient errors with exponential backoff and full jitter; the webhook or a status query decides.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Webhooks: verify, dedupe, move forward">
    Verify the HMAC over the raw body and reject stale timestamps; `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` on the event id in `webhook_events` stops redeliveries. Apply only forward moves (a late "succeeded" after a refund is ignored), and answer `200` fast, duplicates included, or the PSP retries for days.
  </Step>
  <Step title="One writer for each move">
    Sync reply, webhook and recovery job share one function: lock the row, check the transition, then update status, ledger and outbox in one transaction. The first signal wins; the rest find the move illegal. The ledger's unique `source` (`pay_1:charge`) is the last net.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Double-entry ledger">

Each transaction's entries sum to zero, in integer minor units of one currency. $50.00 with a 2.9% + 30¢ PSP fee and 10% commission:

| Account | Type | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash at PSP | asset | $48.25 | |
| PSP fees | expense | $1.75 | |
| Merchant payable (m_42) | liability | | $45.00 |
| Platform revenue | revenue | | $5.00 |
| **Total** | | **$50.00** | **$50.00** |

Append-only (revoke UPDATE and DELETE): refunds and corrections are new transactions. Compute one leg as the remainder (merchant share = amount − commission), so rounding can't create or lose a cent.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Outbox for events">
    Write `payment.succeeded` to the `outbox` table in the same transaction. A relay publishes and marks rows; after a crash it may publish twice, so consumers dedupe by event id. See [the dual-write problem](/learn/async-messaging#the-dual-write-problem-and-the-transactional-outbox).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reconcile every day">
    Match every charge, refund and fee in the PSP's settlement report to the ledger by PSP id and amount, then the PSP's payouts to the bank statement. Fix mismatches with new entries, never by editing history (see the last follow-up).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Refunds and sagas">

One conditional UPDATE reserves a partial refund, so concurrent refunds can't exceed the charge:

```sql
UPDATE payments
SET refunded = refunded + $2,
    status = CASE WHEN refunded + $2 = amount THEN 'refunded' ELSE 'partially_refunded' END
WHERE id = $1 AND status IN ('succeeded', 'partially_refunded') AND refunded + $2 <= amount;
```

Zero rows means `409`. The refund's id is its PSP key; post the ledger on confirmation. Multi-step flows (authorize, reserve stock, capture, pay out) run as a **saga**: every step has a compensation (void, release, refund), all idempotent.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## Failure modes

| What breaks | Effect | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| PSP times out | Charged or not: unknown | `unknown`; same-key retries; webhook, then status query |
| DB down after the PSP charged | Money moved, no record | Intent committed first; webhook retries; recovery job; reconciliation |
| Webhook storm after an outage | Slow handler, more retries | Verify, store, `200` fast; queue with bounded workers |
| Double submit | Two charges | Unique key; `409` in flight; one live payment per order |
| Two partial refunds at once | More refunded than paid | `refunded + $2 <= amount` in the UPDATE; refund id as PSP key |
| Currency rounding | Cents appear or vanish | Integer minor units; remainder leg; exponents (JPY 0, KWD 3) |
| Outbox relay down | Orders not marked paid yet | Rows wait; alert on the oldest unpublished row |

## Trade-offs

| Choice | Pros | Cons | Choose when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sync charge vs async (queue, webhook) | Instant answer; simple | Latency and uptime tied to the PSP | Cards; async for bank debits, 3-D Secure, wallets |
| Ledger vs a balance column | Full history, audits, rebuildable | More rows; balances need aggregating or caching | Always for money; cache balances as a projection |
| Idempotency in the DB vs Redis | Atomic with the payment | Load on the primary | Money; Redis for low-stakes endpoints |
| One PSP vs two | Simple; one reconciliation | A PSP outage stops sales | Most companies; add one for scale or resilience |
| Authorize then capture vs charge now | Capture on shipment; free voids | Authorizations expire (~7 days for cards) | Physical goods; charge now for digital |

## Follow-up questions

<FollowUps>
  <FollowUp q="How do you handle multiple currencies?">
    Tag every entry with its currency, never mix two in one transaction, and convert through an FX clearing account as two linked transactions that store the rate. Reconcile per currency, with each exponent (JPY has no minor unit).
  </FollowUp>
  <FollowUp q="How do payouts to merchants work?">
    A daily job pays each merchant's payable balance minus a reserve for refunds and chargebacks, keyed by merchant plus date, via cash in transit until the bank confirms. Failed payouts reverse with new entries.
  </FollowUp>
  <FollowUp q="Where do fraud checks go?">
    Before the PSP call, under a tight timeout: low risk passes, medium steps up to 3-D Secure (`requires_action`), high is blocked. If the risk service is down, fail open below an amount threshold, closed above.
  </FollowUp>
  <FollowUp q="Can you fail over to a second PSP?">
    Only when PSP A certainly never charged, such as a refused connection: a **timeout** on A then a charge on B can charge twice. Settle A's unknowns through its webhooks and status API, refunding duplicates.
  </FollowUp>
  <FollowUp q="Reconciliation found a mismatch. Now what?">
    Fix forward, never by editing entries: replay a missed webhook from the PSP's events API, reverse a posting that never happened, or adjust for fees and FX. Queue the unexplained for a human.
  </FollowUp>
</FollowUps>
