<TLDR>
Each car is a **state machine** that serves its **up-stops and down-stops** in **LOOK** order, so no floor waits more than two sweeps. One **controller** owns every hall call and hands it to exactly one car through a pluggable **dispatch strategy** (direction-aware nearest car, or collective).
</TLDR>

A **low-level design** (LLD) question with a scheduling problem inside. Split it early: **which car** answers a hall call (dispatch), and **in what order** a car serves its stops (scheduling).

New to low-level design? The dispatch strategy and the car as a state machine are taught in [Swap behaviour](/lld/foundations/strategy-decorator) and [State machines and observers](/lld/foundations/state-observer).

## Clarifying questions

- How many floors and cars? Zones, or every car to every floor?
- An office with a morning rush, a hotel, a hospital with priority calls?
- ▲/▼ buttons per floor, or a destination keypad in the lobby?
- What happens on a car failure, a power cut, a fire alarm?

## Public interface

| Call | Returns | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `hallCall(floor, dir)` | Car id, or `null` if queued | Idempotent: a lit button stays one call |
| `carCall(carId, floor)` | | Adds a stop to that car |
| `tick()` | | Advances every car one step; tests drive it |
| `takeOutOfService(carId)` | | Its hall calls move to other cars |
| `returnToService(carId)` | | Back in the dispatch pool |
| events `assigned`, `queued`, `cleared`, `arrived` | | Lamps, displays and logs observe these |

Each car exposes `addStop(floor, dir?)`, `step()` and `openDoors()`, which throws while moving.

## What it remembers

It all fits in the controller's memory; the snippet is one instant.

```js
// the controller's state at one instant
const state = {
  cars: [
    { id: 'A', floor: 3, dir: 'up', state: 'MOVING_UP', upStops: new Set([5, 7]), downStops: new Set([2]) },
    { id: 'B', floor: 0, dir: null, state: 'IDLE', upStops: new Set(), downStops: new Set() },
  ],
  assigned: new Map([['5▲', { call: { floor: 5, dir: 'up' }, carId: 'A' }]]), // owned exactly when it is here
  waiting: [{ floor: 9, dir: 'down', key: '9▼' }], // no car can take it yet, oldest first
};
```

- A hall call is in exactly one place: `assigned` (with one car) or `waiting`.
- Up calls go into `upStops`, down calls into `downStops`; a car call joins the set on its side of the car.
- Nothing survives a restart: panels re-send lit calls, riders re-press. An append-only event log serves maintenance, not dispatch.

## Step-by-step answer

<Steps>
  <Step title="Use cases and nouns">
    Call, ride, doors, display, failures. Nouns: `ElevatorSystem`, `ElevatorCar`, `Door`, `HallPanel`, `CarPanel`, `Display`, `HallCall`, `CarCall`, plus a `DispatchStrategy` interface.
  </Step>
  <Step title="One car is a state machine">

| From | Event | To |
|---|---|---|
| IDLE | a stop on another floor | MOVING_UP or MOVING_DOWN |
| IDLE | a call at this floor | DOORS_OPEN |
| MOVING_UP / MOVING_DOWN | reached a floor it must stop at | IDLE, then DOORS_OPEN |
| DOORS_OPEN | timer done, sensor clear | IDLE |
| any | fault or maintenance key | OUT_OF_SERVICE |

Safety lives in the car: `openDoors()` throws while moving, `step()` never moves with doors open, and a car that loses its target halts at the next floor rather than reversing in motion.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Two sets of stops per car">
    LOOK asks two questions: is any stop ahead, and is this floor a stop in my direction? A `Set` scan is fine for 20–100 floors; a min-heap of up-stops and a max-heap of down-stops make it O(log n).
  </Step>
  <Step title="LOOK, not FCFS or SCAN">

- **FCFS**: press order, zig-zagging past floors it needs later.
- **Nearest stop first** (SSTF): can starve a far floor.
- **SCAN**: sweeps to the end of the shaft even if nobody is there.
- **LOOK**: sweeps like SCAN, but turns at the last request.

A car never turns while a stop lies ahead, so a waiting floor is passed at most once the wrong way and served within two sweeps: at most 2 × (floors − 1) floors of travel, plus stops.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Dispatch: which car answers">

**Nearest car** picks the lowest **direction-aware** cost: follow the car's sweep to where it can open in the call's direction, plus a penalty per stop first.

```js
// car at 2 sweeping up to 5; call 4▼: up to 5, back to 4 = 4 floors, plus 2 for the stop at 5
cost = routeDistance(car, call.floor, call.dir) + stopCost * stopsBefore;
```

**Collective** assigns nothing up front: a call waits for an idle car or one sweeping past in its direction. Simpler, but cars bunch.

  </Step>
  <Step title="One owner per call">
    Simultaneous presses become messages to one controller loop (an event loop or an actor per building). The first 5▲ is assigned; the second finds it in `assigned` and does nothing. One car per call, no locks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Doors, sensor and load">
    A blocked sensor beam reopens the doors; after ~20 s a slow, buzzing "nudge" close takes over. Above ~80% load the car hands hall calls back to the dispatcher; above 100% it won't leave.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Failures and modes">
    Out of service hands a car's calls to others, keeping their queue place; riders exit at the next floor. Fire service and other modes only change which cars dispatch sees and which stops they accept, so LOOK is untouched.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Displays and lamps">
    Observer: displays, hall lanterns and lamps subscribe to `arrived` and `cleared`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tests">
    Inject the tick. Unit-test LOOK order, a call served in its own direction, the double press and reassignment; then check invariants after every tick of a seeded random run (in the building, no doors open in motion, one owner per call, every call drains within a bound).
  </Step>
</Steps>

- `#plan()` is all of LOOK: stop if this floor is a stop in the sweep direction, continue while anything lies ahead, else turn (serving a call here in the other direction first).
- `routeDistance`: a car at 2 sweeping up to 5 is 2 floors from 4▲, but 4 from 4▼ plus 2 for the stop at 5, so 6.
- `takeOutOfService` re-dispatches at once and puts calls no car can take at the **front** of `waiting`: they were pressed first.

## Capacity estimate

A follow-up, not the opening move: interviewers sometimes ask how many cars a building needs. Size for the **up-peak**, the morning rush when nearly every trip starts in the lobby.

One car's round-trip time (RTT) sets everything.

| Quantity | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Building | 20 floors above the lobby, 3.5 m each (a 70 m rise) |
| Population | 50 per floor = **1,000** |
| Target | 12% of them in the busiest 5 minutes = **120 people** |
| Load per trip | A 13-person car, 80% full: **P = 10** |
| Travel | 2.5 m/s: **1.4 s per floor** (3.5 ÷ 2.5) |
| Stops per trip | S = 20 × (1 − (19/20)^10) ≈ **8.0** (10 riders, some sharing a floor) |
| Highest floor reached | H ≈ **18.6** on average |
| Time per stop | ~**8 s**: close 3 s, open 2 s, ~3 s braking and accelerating |
| Transfer | **1.2 s** per passenger, in at the lobby, out upstairs |

RTT = 2 × H × 1.4 + (S + 1) × 8 + 2 × P × 1.2 = 52 + 72 + 24 ≈ **148 s**.

- One car: 300 × 10 ÷ 148 ≈ **20 people per 5 minutes**.
- **Four cars**: about **81**, leaving the lobby every 148 ÷ 4 ≈ **37 s** (offices aim for under 30 s).
- **Six cars**: about 121, every ~25 s. At 1.6 m/s instead of 2.5, the RTT is about 178 s and six cars move about 101.

The surprise: **stopping (72 s) costs more than travelling (52 s)**. Destination dispatch's 4 stops instead of 8 give an RTT of about 116 s: four cars then move about 103. The controller is tiny (120 presses in 5 minutes is 0.4 per second, plus a tick per car every 50–100 ms), so correctness is the hard part. The timeline plays one trip.

## Failure modes

| What breaks | Effect | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Something blocks the doors | Car stuck | Sensor reopens; nudge close after ~20 s, then a fault |
| A car fails | Its calls wait forever | Calls go to other cars; riders exit at the next floor |
| Power cut | Car stops between floors | Brakes hold; battery rescue drives it to the nearest floor and opens |
| Position or load sensor fault | Car doesn't know where it is | Stop, level at the next floor, go out of service; never guess |
| Overload | Car can't leave safely | No departure over capacity; bypass hall calls above ~80% |
| Controller crash | Assignments lost | Panels re-send; cars serve calls on their own |
| Fire alarm | Lifts become dangerous | Recall to the designated floor, doors open; firefighter control only |

## Trade-offs

| Choice | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| FCFS | Trivial; fair by arrival (a baseline) | Zig-zags; waits grow fast |
| Nearest car (direction-aware) | Short waits; the default for ▲/▼ buildings | Commits at press time |
| Collective | No assignment state; fine for small groups | Cars bunch; calls may wait |
| Destination dispatch | Fewer stops: tall offices with a heavy rush | Keypads; no changing your mind |
| One controller | No double service | Single point of failure (cars fall back to local mode) |

<Callout kind="interview" title="What interviewers listen for">
A car **state machine** with safety inside; **dispatch** split from **scheduling**; LOOK with a waiting bound; **one owner per hall call**; RTT numbers behind the car count. A `class Elevator` with `goTo(floor)` and no direction loses points.
</Callout>

## Follow-up questions

<FollowUps>
  <FollowUp q="How would destination dispatch change the design?">
    Riders key in their floor in the lobby, so `HallCall` becomes a `DestinationRequest` (`from`, `to`) behind the same strategy, and grouping by floor cuts stops, the biggest slice of the round trip.
  </FollowUp>
  <FollowUp q="How would you add VIP and fire-service modes?">
    Modes are system state: a VIP key pulls one car out of dispatch, and fire recall parks every car at the designated floor, doors open, for firefighter control. LOOK is untouched.
  </FollowUp>
  <FollowUp q="How would you scale this to many buildings?">
    Keep dispatch local (safety can't depend on a network) and stream events to a central service, sharded by building id, for dashboards and predictive maintenance.
  </FollowUp>
  <FollowUp q="How would you save energy or prepare for the morning rush?">
    Park idle cars by policy (the lobby before 9 am, some off at night) and add an energy term so a moving car beats waking a parked one.
  </FollowUp>
  <FollowUp q="How do you test it?">
    Inject the tick and property-test the invariants over seeded random runs; replaying one seed compares strategies fairly, as the simulator does.
  </FollowUp>
</FollowUps>
