<TLDR>
Each car is a **state machine** that serves stops in **LOOK** order, so no floor waits more than two sweeps. One **controller** gives each hall call to exactly one car through a swappable **dispatch strategy**.
</TLDR>

A **low-level design** (LLD) question with two problems inside. **Dispatch**: which car answers a call. **Scheduling**: in what order a car serves its stops.

New to this? Read [Swap behaviour](/lld/foundations/strategy-decorator) and [State machines and observers](/lld/foundations/state-observer) first.

## Public interface

| Call | Returns | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `hallCall(floor, dir)` | Car id, or `null` if queued | Idempotent: a lit button stays one call |
| `carCall(carId, floor)` | | Adds a stop to that car |
| `tick()` | | Advances every car one step; tests drive it |
| `takeOutOfService(carId)` | | Its hall calls move to other cars |
| `returnToService(carId)` | | Back in the dispatch pool |
| events `assigned`, `queued`, `cleared`, `arrived` | | Lamps, displays and logs observe these |

Each car exposes `addStop(floor, dir?)`, `step()` and `openDoors()`, which throws while moving.

## What it remembers

All of it fits in the controller's memory. This is one instant.

```js
// the controller's state at one instant
const state = {
  cars: [
    { id: 'A', floor: 3, dir: 'up', state: 'MOVING_UP', upStops: new Set([5, 7]), downStops: new Set([2]) },
    { id: 'B', floor: 0, dir: null, state: 'IDLE', upStops: new Set(), downStops: new Set() },
  ],
  assigned: new Map([['5▲', { call: { floor: 5, dir: 'up' }, carId: 'A' }]]), // owned exactly when it is here
  waiting: [{ floor: 9, dir: 'down', key: '9▼' }], // no car can take it yet, oldest first
};
```

- A hall call is in exactly one place: `assigned` (to one car) or `waiting`.
- Up calls go to `upStops`, down calls to `downStops`.
- After a restart, panels re-send lit calls.

## Step-by-step answer

<Steps>
  <Step title="Use cases and nouns">
    Call, ride, doors, display, failures. Nouns: `ElevatorSystem`, `ElevatorCar`, `HallCall`, `CarCall`, `DispatchStrategy`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="One car is a state machine">

| From | Event | To |
|---|---|---|
| IDLE | a stop on another floor | MOVING_UP or MOVING_DOWN |
| IDLE | a call at this floor | DOORS_OPEN |
| MOVING_UP / MOVING_DOWN | reached a floor it must stop at | IDLE, then DOORS_OPEN |
| DOORS_OPEN | timer done, sensor clear | IDLE |
| any | fault or maintenance key | OUT_OF_SERVICE |

Safety lives in the car: `openDoors()` throws while moving, and `step()` never moves with doors open.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Two sets of stops per car">
    LOOK asks: is any stop ahead, and is this floor a stop in my direction?
  </Step>
  <Step title="LOOK, not FCFS or SCAN">

- **FCFS**: press order, zig-zagging.
- **Nearest stop first**: can starve a far floor.
- **LOOK**: sweeps up, then down, and turns at the last request.

A car never turns while a stop lies ahead, so every floor is served within two sweeps.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Dispatch: which car answers">

**Nearest car** picks the lowest cost, following each car's sweep, plus a penalty per stop on the way.

```js
// car at 2 sweeping up to 5; call 4▼: up to 5, back to 4 = 4 floors, plus 2 for the stop at 5
cost = routeDistance(car, call.floor, call.dir) + stopCost * stopsBefore;
```

**Collective** assigns nothing up front. A call waits for a car passing its way. Simpler, but cars bunch.

  </Step>
  <Step title="One owner per call">
    Presses are messages to one controller loop. The first 5▲ is assigned. The second finds it in `assigned` and does nothing. No locks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Doors, sensor and load">
    A blocked sensor reopens the doors. Over ~80% load the car skips hall calls.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Failures and modes">
    A failed car hands its calls to others. Fire service only changes which cars dispatch can use.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Displays and lamps">
    Displays and lamps listen for `arrived` and `cleared` (an **observer**).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tests">
    Inject the tick. Test LOOK order and the double press. Check after every tick: no open doors in motion, one owner per call.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Capacity estimate

A follow-up: how many cars does a building need? Size for the **up-peak**, the morning rush when every trip starts in the lobby. One car's round-trip time (RTT) sets everything.

| Quantity | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Building | 20 floors, 3.5 m each |
| People | 50 per floor = **1,000**; 12% in the busiest 5 minutes = **120** |
| Load per trip | 13-person car, 80% full: **P = 10** |
| Travel | 2.5 m/s: **1.4 s per floor** |
| Stops per trip | **S ≈ 8** (10 riders, some share a floor) |
| Time per stop | ~**8 s** (doors and braking) |
| Transfer | **1.2 s** per passenger |

RTT = travel 52 + stops 72 + transfers 24 ≈ **148 s**.

- One car: 300 × 10 ÷ 148 ≈ **20 people per 5 minutes**.
- **Four cars**: about **81**, one leaving the lobby every ~37 s (offices aim for under 30 s).
- **Six cars**: about 121, every ~25 s.

The surprise: **stopping (72 s) costs more than travelling (52 s)**. Destination dispatch halves the stops to 4, so four cars then move about 103. The controller is tiny (0.4 presses a second), so correctness is the hard part. The timeline plays one trip.

## Failure modes

| What breaks | Effect | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Doors blocked | Car stuck | Sensor reopens; a slow close after ~20 s, then a fault |
| A car fails | Its calls wait forever | Calls go to other cars; riders exit at the next floor |
| Power cut | Car stops between floors | Brakes hold; battery drives it to the nearest floor |
| Sensor fault | Car loses its position | Stop at the next floor, go out of service |
| Overload | Unsafe to leave | No departure over capacity |
| Controller crash | Assignments lost | Panels re-send; cars serve calls alone |
| Fire alarm | Lifts are dangerous | Recall to the designated floor, doors open |

## Trade-offs

| Choice | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| FCFS | Trivial and fair | Zig-zags; long waits |
| Nearest car | Short waits | Commits at press time |
| Collective | No state to keep | Cars bunch |
| Destination dispatch | Fewer stops | Needs keypads |
| One controller | No double service | Single point of failure |

## Follow-up questions

<FollowUps>
  <FollowUp q="How would destination dispatch change the design?">
    Riders key in their floor, so `HallCall` becomes a `DestinationRequest` (`from`, `to`) behind the same strategy. Grouping by floor cuts stops.
  </FollowUp>
  <FollowUp q="How would you add VIP and fire-service modes?">
    Modes are system state. A VIP key pulls one car out of dispatch. Fire recall parks every car with doors open. LOOK is untouched.
  </FollowUp>
  <FollowUp q="How would you scale this to many buildings?">
    Keep dispatch local, since safety can't depend on a network. Stream events to a central service, split by building, for dashboards.
  </FollowUp>
  <FollowUp q="How would you save energy or prepare for the morning rush?">
    Park idle cars by policy (the lobby before 9 am). Add an energy cost so a moving car beats waking a parked one.
  </FollowUp>
  <FollowUp q="How do you test it?">
    Inject the tick and check the rules over seeded random runs. Replaying one seed compares strategies fairly.
  </FollowUp>
</FollowUps>
