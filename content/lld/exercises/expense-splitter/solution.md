Money has two hard parts: the stray cent, and the fact that "who owes whom" can be recorded in more than one way. The design keeps both small by making the split a swappable rule and the ledger a plain map.

1. **Make each split type a function.** `split(amount, users, data)` returns one share per user, or nothing when the data is invalid. A table maps `"equal"`, `"exact"`, `"percent"` and `"shares"` to their functions, and `addExpense` looks the type up. A fifth type is one new entry, and no other line changes. That is the Strategy pattern without any class ceremony.
2. **Share one rounding routine.** Equal, percent and shares are all "proportional to weights", so one `spread(amount, weights)` floors each share and then hands the leftover cents out one each to the first users in order. Equal is the case where every weight is 1. Exact does not need rounding, only a check that it adds up.
3. **Validate before you write.** Check the members, the amount, the type and the data first, and only then add shares to the ledger. A rejected expense then cannot leave a half-applied bill behind, and `expenseCount` only counts accepted ones.
4. **Store debts per ordered pair, netted.** Adding "u owes p cents" first cancels any debt the other way: `net = owes(u, p) + cents - owes(p, u)`, then store `max(0, net)` one way and `max(0, -net)` the other. The `+ owes(u, p)` term matters: without it the second dinner between the same two people overwrites the first.
5. **Compute balances and simplify from the ledger.** `balance(user)` adds what is owed to them and subtracts what they owe. Nothing else is stored, so there is no second copy that can disagree.
6. **Simplify on nets, with fixed tie-breaks.** Collect debtors and creditors, sort each by amount (largest first, then by name), pay the smaller of the two front amounts, and drop whoever reaches 0. Every payment clears at least one person, so n people need at most n - 1 payments. The result depends on the net balances only, which is why the chain "Ann owes Ben, Ben owes Cam" becomes "Ann pays Cam".
7. **Settle with a limit.** A settle that exceeds the debt would flip its direction by accident, so it is rejected, along with strangers, self-payments and non-positive amounts.

Where the design bends: currencies mean one ledger per currency; a tip or tax is a decorator that adds to the amount before the strategy runs; undo is a new reversing entry. In a real app each group gets one lock, and `settle` must check and subtract inside it so two taps cannot both pay the same debt.

Cost: adding an expense is O(n) for n participants. This simplify re-sorts on every step, which is O(n² log n) and fine for a friend group. A real version keeps two heaps and does O(n log n). The true minimum number of payments is NP-hard, and the greedy answer is not always the smallest.
