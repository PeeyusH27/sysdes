Two separate ideas hide in this problem, and the design is mostly about keeping them apart: a **state machine** for the session, and a **planner** for the cash.

1. **Name the states and what each accepts.** `IDLE` accepts only `insertCard`; `CARD_IN` accepts `enterPin` and `eject`; `AUTHENTICATED` accepts `balance`, `withdraw` and `eject`. Put that in a table of handlers per state. Anything not in the table gets the sentinel the statement gives for it (`INVALID_STATE`, or `-1` for `balance`), and changes nothing. There is no `pinOk` flag to forget to clear.
2. **Keep the PIN count on the account.** Failed tries are a property of the account, not of one session: they survive `eject` and a new card insertion, and a correct PIN resets them. The third wrong PIN sets `locked`, drops the card and returns to `IDLE`. Locked is a flag on the account, not a fourth machine state, because `insertCard` is where it is checked.
3. **Make the note planner a pure function.** `plan(amount, stock)` returns the notes or nothing, and it never changes the stock. A chain of handlers works well: each owns one note value, tries the most notes it can, and asks the next handler to pay the rest. If the rest cannot be paid it tries one note fewer. That backtracking is what makes 60 come out as three 20s when only one 50 is left.
4. **Get the preference rule for free.** Trying "most notes first" from 100 downwards produces exactly the order the statement asks for: most 100s, then most 50s, and so on. No scoring is needed.
5. **Check in the stated order, and only then commit.** Validate the amount, plan the notes, check the balance, check the jam, and only after all of those subtract the money and the notes. A failure before the commit has nothing to undo, so a rejected withdrawal can never change a balance or the stock.
6. **Order matters for tests.** With a balance of 40 and only 50-notes in the machine, 70 must say `NO_EXACT_NOTES` and not `INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`. Planning comes before the balance check by design: it is pure and cheap.

Where the design bends: a new note value is one more handler (or one more number in a loop); a deposit is one more event in `AUTHENTICATED`; a daily limit is one more check before the commit. The chain is more structure than four fixed notes need, and a loop over an array with the same backtracking would also be a good answer. It pays off if handlers gain their own rules, such as a per-transaction cap on one note.

In a real ATM the debit and the cash are two steps that cannot be one transaction. You would debit, ask the hardware to dispense, and credit back if it fails. Here the jam is known before anything is committed, which stands in for that undo, and the order is the point: a failure never leaves money taken and no cash paid. Two machines sharing one account would debit under a lock on the account, so that two checks of the balance cannot both pass.

Cost: every event is O(1) except planning, which tries at most a few hundred combinations for four note values.
