<TLDR>
Use the same steps every time: **requirements, numbers, API and data model, design, two deep dives, trade-offs**, in about 45 minutes. Say every choice as "X because Y, at the cost of Z". When stuck, go back to requirements and numbers.
</TLDR>

## Why it matters

Knowing the pieces is half the job. Interviewers score scope, numbers, a working design, depth and trade-offs. Fixed steps cover every row even when you are nervous.

## The 45-minute plan

Five phases: **5, 5, 15, 15 and 5 minutes**. Press play to fill them in.

Behind? Say so and move on: "I'll keep the data model brief so we have time for the deep dives."

## The framework as a checklist

Each step leaves something on the board for the next. Play **Getting unstuck** to see how to rescue a stalled answer.

<FlowDiagram spec={interviewFramework} />

<Steps>
  <Step title="Requirements">
    3 to 5 features, **numbers** for speed, uptime and scale, and what is out of scope.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Estimation">
    Peak requests a second and storage. Then say what it **implies**: "read-heavy, so cache."
  </Step>
  <Step title="API">
    The 3 to 5 endpoints that matter.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Data model">
    Tables or keys, picked by how you read them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="High-level design">
    The simplest boxes that work. Walk one request end to end.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deep dives">
    The two riskiest areas, and **what happens when they fail**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Trade-offs and wrap-up">
    What each choice cost, then a 30-second summary.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Frontend variant: RADIO

Frontend rounds use the same idea in five stages.

| Letter | Stage | What to cover |
|---|---|---|
| R | Requirements | Features, devices, offline, speed targets |
| A | Architecture | Component tree, where state lives |
| D | Data model | Client state shape, what is cached |
| I | Interface | Props, events, endpoints |

## What interviewers look for

Every level gets the same question. The bar is what moves.

| Signal | Mid | Senior | Staff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Requirements | Asks some questions | Drives scope, adds numbers | Reframes the problem |
| Design | A working design | Clean data flow, scaling | Evolution path, operations |
| Deep dive | One area | Two areas, with failures | The riskiest area, quantified |
| Trade-offs | Names some | Justifies each | Cost and team impact |

## Talking through trade-offs

Interviewers score reasoning, not a perfect answer. Use this shape:

"I'll use **X** because **Y**. The cost is **Z**. If **W** changes, I'd switch to **V**."

For example: "I'll return a **302** because we promised click analytics. The cost: every click reaches our servers."

Let the numbers pick the moment to stop. 40 writes a second: "One Postgres is enough, so no sharding." Roughly 40,000: "Now I'd shard by a hash of the code."

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| The prompt is vague | "Who uses it, and how many?" |
| Your numbers come out small | "One database is enough. I'll skip sharding." |
| You are asked about failure | "If this box dies, X takes over, detected by Y." |
| You are behind | "I'll keep this brief so we reach the deep dives." |

## Drawing conventions

Your board is how the interviewer follows you.

- One shape each for services, data stores and queues.
- **Label every arrow** with what flows.
- **Number the request flow**, so you can say "step 4".
- Keep your assumptions in a corner box.

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="warning">
- Jumping to tools ("Kafka, Cassandra") before the requirements.
- Over-engineering: multi-region for 40 writes a second.
- Ignoring failure: every box can die, every call can time out.
</Callout>

Prepare two lines:

- **Pushback**: "Good point. If that matters, I'd change..." It is a hint, not an attack.
- **A gap**: "I don't know X in detail, but here is how I'd reason about it."

## Practice plan

Work in this order, aloud and on a timer: [URL shortener](/learn/case-url-shortener), [rate limiter](/questions/rate-limiter), [notification system](/learn/case-notification-system), [chat](/questions/chat), [news feed](/questions/news-feed), then payments.

- **Redo each after 3, 7 and 21 days.**
- **Record yourself.** Playback exposes silences and skipped numbers.
- **Use the [playground](/playground/url-shortener)** to get graded.
- **Score every run** 1 to 4 per row below.

| Dimension | 1: needs work | 4: strong |
|---|---|---|
| Requirements | Starts drawing at once | Scope and numbers agreed by minute 5 |
| Estimation | No numbers | QPS, storage, and what they imply |
| API and data model | Vague endpoints | Clear endpoints, keys, access patterns |
| High-level design | Boxes, no flow | A numbered flow, end to end |
| Deep dives | Stays on the surface | Two areas in depth |
| Failure modes | Not mentioned | What breaks, how it is detected and recovers |
| Trade-offs | Choices stated as facts | Each choice's cost, and when to switch |
| Communication | Draws in silence | Thinks aloud, manages the clock |

## Interview questions

<FollowUps>
  <FollowUp q="How do you handle an ambiguous problem?">
    Ask questions, state assumptions, and confirm before designing. They want to see you narrow scope.
  </FollowUp>
  <FollowUp q="The interviewer pushes back on your choice. What do you do?">
    Treat it as a hint: restate the concern, then defend with numbers or adapt.
  </FollowUp>
  <FollowUp q="How deep should you go?">
    Broad until the design works end to end; then deep where the risk is highest.
  </FollowUp>
  <FollowUp q="What if you get stuck?">
    Back to requirements and numbers, simplify, then scale step by step out loud.
  </FollowUp>
</FollowUps>
